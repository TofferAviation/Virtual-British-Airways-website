# Terrain and NOAA aurora integration (2026-10-23)

Open `http://127.0.0.1:3107/dev/globe`. This remains a local change; it has not been deployed.

## Ground and terrain

The camera has a **Go to ground · 5 m** control. Wheel and Q/E can also descend. Clearance is checked against the same triangular DEM interpolation used by the displayed mesh, not an assumed spherical Earth. On approach, unknown or insufficient local terrain holds descent at 9.1 km until a valid tile is ready. A temporary data gap beneath an already low eye holds its current height instead of launching it back up to 9.1 km. Unsupported polar terrain never permits a new ground descent. Initially requested low-altitude views start safely above unknown terrain and restore the requested altitude smoothly once the DEM is ready.

Vertical movement now uses a clearance-scaled velocity/acceleration limit, integrated in small bounded time steps, instead of the previous fast position-only damping. Blocked wheel/key input cannot bank a much lower target and cause a catch-up dive when height data arrives. Explicit Go to ground remains an automatic request, but starts/restarts through the same motion limiter; its view tilt eases as well. Manual reverse gestures take effect without waiting for an old target to finish. Genuine newly discovered terrain collisions can still require immediate upward safety correction; terrain rendering itself has not been replaced by a temporal DEM morph.

Collision-detail prefetch starts on the approach (under 80 km when detail is missing, or with a near-ground target), rather than only at the 9.1 km barrier. DEM work takes one existing request slot ahead of background imagery. Under cache pressure, unloaded siblings borrow imagery and non-focus images can be safely rebound to their parents to make room for the local collision chain. Roots and the camera's own image ancestry are retained. Cancelled obsolete DEM requests no longer impose a 30-second retry penalty. Existing concurrency, tile and memory caps remain unchanged.

Near-ground wheel and keyboard speed use clearance above terrain rather than mountain elevation, so a small zoom over a 3 km mountain does not jump hundreds of metres. Eye positions use full geodetic height, not a radial approximation that shifts the eye sideways into steep terrain.

Mapzen Terrarium tiles from AWS Open Data are decoded as numeric heights (R × 256 + G + B/256 − 32768), resampled into 65 × 65 geographic height grids, and applied at real scale over WGS84. Source elevations use source geoid datums: no survey-grade geoid-to-ellipsoid correction is claimed. Bathymetry is held at sea level. The Mercator source does not cover latitudes beyond ±85.05112878°; those caps remain ellipsoidal.

Tile-local vertices plus an eye-relative world origin preserve close-up precision. Logarithmic depth, inward LOD skirts, updated normals, real terrain depth for cloud clipping and cloud-shadow sampling, and depth-tested atmosphere prevent terrain penetration, cracks and sky/cloud overlays across ridges.

DEM requests share the existing client concurrency limit (one DEM in flight), reserve future geometry memory, abort obsolete work and dispose released buffers. The fixed-origin server proxy allows only validated z/x/y coordinates, caps upstream concurrency at three, and retains at most 64 decoded 256² DEM tiles (~16 MiB). Geometry can refine to LOD 14 (Low: 12); imagery stops at LOD 10 and is correctly cropped/reused above its native level. Historical Landsat is approximately 30 m imagery, not ground-level photogrammetry, buildings or trees.

Low resizes only the two distant fallback roots to 512², preserving locally downloaded detail. Ground mode prioritises the collision tile over distant horizon refinement. Under cache pressure, finer DEM meshes can borrow a correctly cropped parent image rather than waiting for four new texture allocations; higher quality opportunistically upgrades borrowed imagery when budget allows. Split estimates are recounted before network scheduling so unused sibling reservations cannot starve elevation requests. The scheduler regression explicitly descends in Low within its 48 MiB budget and then upgrades/downgrades without holes or request-budget violations.

Provider attribution and limitations are published at `/globe-sources`.

## Aurora

The complete operational NOAA grid is validated, including quiet/zero cells, coordinate order, unique cells, timestamps, regular one-degree coverage and both hemispheres. Unsupported resolution/schema changes fail closed. The last verified snapshot survives refresh errors in the server's process cache and is marked stale. This is not persistent storage across a server restart. Refresh is every five minutes, with one-minute retry backoff after upstream failure. Older observation snapshots are rejected.

The GPU receives a periodic 360 × 181 activity texture. Activity-weighted latitude quantiles place **finite regional arcs**, not four continuous globe-spanning ribbons. Low has 192 arcs across both hemispheres; Medium/High have 288. Their centres, lengths, latitude tracks, hooks and independent seeds are stable across frames and forecast updates. Long arcs overlap shorter structures; tapered ends and evolving gaps prevent a handful of rectangular sections from defining the display. NOAA is sampled at the displaced geographic footprint, so procedural movement cannot illuminate quiet cells.

The arcs are batched into eight meshes at every quality level, with no per-frame CPU geometry updates or object allocation for animation. Profile geometry costs are approximately 4.35 MiB Low, 10.22 MiB Medium and 14.68 MiB High (attributes plus indices). Quality scales tessellation rather than making hundreds of separate draw calls; emission weights are normalised by lane count so extra detail does not increase total source weight. Forecast updates morph existing buffers and blend activity over 12 seconds; the vertex shader corrects chord interpolation back to the intended emission altitude. Culling bounds include both forecast endpoints plus motion padding.

Quintic-interpolated space/time noise drives independent lifecycles, travelling folds, lateral curls and brightness knots. Horizontal movement deforms the geometry in two directions; weak upper-curtain shear retains the dominant WMM inclination. Uneven lower hems and varying ray-top heights break the previous level band/comb pattern. Ray coordinates are fixed in arc-distance space: zoom changes derivative filtering, not the geographic noise frequency. This is coherent seeded procedural motion, not frame-to-frame random regeneration, nor a claim that NOAA predicts individual folds.

Vertical emission now uses overlapping, independently seeded ray packets with separate event strengths, rise/decay envelopes, peak heights and travelling emission fronts. Events begin/end at zero amplitude with quintic envelopes, including when their next seed is selected; compact spatial support prevents seams at packet-cell boundaries. The previous strong uniform body has been reduced so individual bundles can actually dissolve into gaps. Fine filaments modulate these packets instead of defining the whole curtain. Derivative filtering suppresses unresolved packet variation in distant views. The red upper glow has a separate slower modulation, rather than flashing in lockstep with the lower border. Packet timing is artistic procedural animation, not measured plasma motion or a new NOAA prediction product.

Inside the auroral altitude range the camera keeps a 10 m near plane (with the existing logarithmic depth). The previous quarter-altitude orbit near plane could slice nearby curtains tens of kilometres away, exposing straight-edged panes when the eye entered them. Other orbit and ground clipping behaviour is unchanged.

Full degree/order 12 WMM2025 with secular variation inclines rays using a bounded 4° direction lattice. NOAA geographic activity is **not rotated again** or expanded using Kp. WMM2025 passes NOAA's 100 official field-component test vectors to within 0.2 nT. Model validity is 2025–2029 and −1 to 850 km; expired epochs use explicitly documented local-vertical geometry instead of extrapolated magnetic results. This is local field alignment, not a full magnetospheric field-line/particle simulation.

Emission spans 95–450 km: a broad green oxygen profile, fainter high-altitude red oxygen and restrained lower nitrogen colour. Spatial noise generates evolving nonuniform rays; derivative filtering suppresses subpixel shimmer. Animation speed and pause use an accumulated clock, not a phase reset. Both hemispheres remain independent. Atmosphere attenuates emission without adding its sky term twice. Opaque terrain occludes far-side curtains; below-cloud views composite aurora before clouds so cloud opacity masks it. From orbit it is composited above the troposphere. Within the cloud volume, a single before/after switch at 18 km is an approximation, not a per-sample unified cloud/aurora integrator.

Rendering is physically inspired, not calibrated spectral radiance or a plasma simulation. Display-RGB spectral approximations and additive transparency are used; there is no full-frame HDR radiometric composition, OIT or added bloom pass. Enhanced photography changes emission exposure only, never coverage or altitude. NOAA does not observe/predict the individual displayed folds, rays or colour.

## Calmer timing and opt-in manual Kp (23 October)

The later timing request changes the shared default to **30% of the previous clock speed**, without reintroducing the rejected vortex or changing the accepted palette, altitude profiles or curtain topology. Each bundle has a bounded monotonic local clock, separately seeded rise/hold/release timings and brief quiet gaps. The vertical bright front eases at its endpoints; fine filament drift is slower and varies gently per arc. Existing folds retain their spatial function and simply run on the slower master clock. Pause and speed adjustments preserve phase; frame stalls cannot fast-forward motion. These are physically inspired artistic dynamics, not NOAA-measured individual ray motion. The live page and QA both expose the motion slider.

`Manual Kp simulation` is independent of screenshot colour gains. It is off by default; enabling it allows integer Kp 0–9 (zero is not interpreted as Live). Higher settings generate an illustrative brighter/broader/equatorward oval about a tilted dipole. The coefficients are art-directed, **not an empirical Kp-to-OVATION or visibility model**. Live OVATION data is never rotated, expanded, overwritten or published as simulation data. The preview works without an available live feed. A separate grid is generated once per committed selection, then uses the existing eight batches and two forecast textures. Source changes use the existing 12-second field/geometry blend, preserving buffers; returning to Live selects the latest real field, or fails closed if no live data exists. Simulation diagnostics carry no NOAA forecast/observation timestamps. Caption/control/QA banners mark the simulated Kp, and recording filenames capture the source setting at recording start. No Kp override is saved to a pilot profile or sent to the weather API.

Verification now includes **54 globe tests plus nine cloud tests**, TypeScript, targeted ESLint and the production build. Added regressions cover slow frame-rate-independent timing, pause/stall/phase safety, independent bounded local tempos, reseed/spatial continuity under the warped clock, lower per-frame vertical/brightness changes, Kp opt-in/zero/bounds, deterministic dual-hemisphere/seam-safe grids, qualitative equatorward expansion, source immutability and reuse of actual geometry through simulated/live changes.
